# Outline the article in attention-staged layers

Plan the shape before you draft the prose. First answer, for yourself, the only question
that matters: **why would someone read this?** If you cannot name what is genuinely
interesting or consequential here, the piece is not ready, go back to research.

Plan the article in layers, so a reader gets value at every depth and can stop anywhere:

1. **Hook (the dek):** one line that sharpens the headline and sells why this matters. Not
   the headline reworded.
2. **Summary:** a few sentences that deliver the whole story densely. A reader who stops
   here still knows what happened and why it matters. Highest information per word.
3. **More about this:** the developing detail, the key facts, numbers, names, and the one
   or two competing claims the summary could only gesture at.
4. **Full context:** the background, history, and second-order consequences for the reader
   who wants the whole thing. Close here.

Each layer carries NEW, dense information; no layer restates an earlier one in more words.
Do not label the layers in the prose and do not let it read like a template; the staging is
felt in the writing, not announced.

Produce a section-by-section outline mapped onto these layers: for each section a short
heading and one line on what it covers and which ledger source(s) it draws on. Outline
only; do not write the prose yet. There is no limit on how many sections you plan.
